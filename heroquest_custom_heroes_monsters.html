Custom Heroes & Monsters

How to design your own heroes and monsters as house rules, starting from the official stat lines and checking balance with exact dice odds.

The quest book itself invites you to build new quests on the blank map at the back, and several Dread spells are left for quests you design. Custom heroes and monsters are the next step. Everything on this page is a house rule: agree on it with your group before you play, and don't mix it up with the official rules.

What You're Working With

HeroQuest describes every figure with a handful of numbers. Heroes have Attack dice, Defend dice, Body Points and Mind Points, and they move by rolling two red dice. Monsters have the same four numbers plus a fixed movement. Keeping that small set is the whole trick: if your creation needs a page of rules, it no longer feels like HeroQuest.

The Official Heroes

These four starting stat lines are the same in the classic and 2021 editions, and they're the best yardstick for any new hero.

The official pack heroes (Knight, Rogue, Monk and the rest) each add a special ability instead of breaking these numbers wide open; see The Heroes.

What One Die Is Worth

Before you hand out extra dice, know their value. A combat die has 3 skull faces, 2 white shields and 1 black shield. Heroes block with white shields (1 in 3) and monsters block with black shields (1 in 6), so an extra Attack die is worth roughly twice what an extra Defend die is worth.

Designing a Hero

House rule: nothing in this section is official. It's a method for making your own hero that sits comfortably beside the official ones.

🛡️ Start From an Official Hero

Begin with the story: who is this person, and what will they do that the party can't already do? Then pick the official hero closest to that idea and change as little as possible.

  • Movement stays at two red dice. Every hero moves this way; a hero who moves faster than everyone else breaks the pace of the game. If you want a quick hero, give a limited ability instead.
  • Keep Defend at 2. Every official hero starts there; armor from the armory is how heroes improve it.
  • Trade, don't add. Keep Body plus Mind around 10. If Attack goes up, something else should go down.
  • One ability, with a limit. "Once per quest" is the cleanest limit HeroQuest has, and it matches how spells work.

Example: The Sellsword (house rule)

Attack: 2 (shortsword) · Defend: 2 · Body: 7 · Mind: 3 · Movement: two red dice

Ability, Hold the Line: once per quest, when a monster attacks a hero adjacent to the Sellsword, the Sellsword may take the attack instead and defends with 1 extra Defend die.

Limit: can't cast spells.

This is the Dwarf's stat line with trap-disarming swapped for a protective ability. From the table above, the extra die cuts an orc's average damage from 0.96 to 0.75 Body Points for that one attack. The real value is moving the hit from the Wizard (4 Body) to a 7-Body hero.

🔄 Borrowing From Other Games

Don't convert numbers from another game; their scales don't map onto HeroQuest's handful of dice. Convert the role instead. A tough front-line fighter from another game becomes a variant of the Barbarian or Dwarf. A spellcaster becomes a variant of the Elf or Wizard, using the existing spell schools. A sneaky character gets a once-per-quest trick rather than better stats.

Designing a Monster

House rule: custom monsters go in your own quest notes. Write their stats there, just as the official quests do for named villains.

👹 Fixed Movement Shapes the Threat

Heroes roll two dice to move, so their speed varies. Monsters have fixed movement, which the heroes can learn and plan around. On the classic chart a Mummy moves 4 squares and a Goblin moves 10. Slow monsters tend to be tough, and fast ones tend to be fragile; follow the same pattern.

🐀 Fodder

Like a Goblin or Skeleton: Body 1, Attack 2, Defend 1–2. Dies to a single good hit. Use several.

⚔️ Soldier

Like an Orc or Abomination: Attack 3, Defend 2–3, Body 1–2. The backbone of most rooms.

🛡️ Elite

Like a Dread Warrior or Gargoyle: Attack 4, Defend 4–5, Body 3. One per room is plenty.

👑 Named villain

Give a named villain its own stat box in your quest notes and, if you like, a Dread spell or two.

Example: Crystal Wraith (house rule)

Move: 6 · Attack: 2 · Defend: 3 · Body: 2 · Mind: 4

Special: may move through walls (but must end its move on an empty square).

Weakness: can't cast spells and never guards treasure.

Check the balance: a Barbarian (3 Attack dice) kills it in one attack 575/1728 of the time (33.3%), and within two attacks 68.1% of the time. A Wizard with a dagger (1 Attack die) barely scratches it. Its 2-die attack against a hero with 2 Defend dice averages 5/9 of a Body Point. It's a nuisance that harasses weaker heroes, not a killer, which is right for a monster that can come through walls.

Keeping It Fair

⚖️ How Heroes Really Grow

HeroQuest heroes don't have levels or experience points. Their starting numbers stay the same; they get stronger by buying weapons and armor with the gold they find, and by carrying potions and artifacts. So don't design a custom hero who "levels up". If you want progression, tie it to gear the hero can buy between quests.

Common Balance Mistakes

Power creep: each new creation is slightly better than the last. Compare against the four official heroes, not against your previous custom hero.

One solution only: a monster only one hero can hurt makes the rest of the table watch.

No weakness: every official hero has an obvious gap, like the Wizard's 4 Body Points or the Barbarian's 2 Mind Points. Yours should too.

Forgetting equipment: a hero who already starts with 3 Attack dice and then buys a better weapon may outclass everyone.

Balance Checklist

  • Does it trade a strength for a weakness?
  • Does it fill a role no current hero fills?
  • How does it change when it gets the best armory gear?
  • Can it help in most quests, not just one kind?
  • Does it give the player interesting choices?
  • Does it feel like it belongs in HeroQuest?

Playtesting

Play several different quests with the new hero or monster before you call it finished.

Ask whether it was fun, not just whether it was fair.

Try the extremes: best possible gear, worst luck, the worst matchup.

🎯 Try It at the Table

  1. Take the Elf's stat line and design a hero who trades the Elf's spells for a once-per-quest ability. Use the dice tables above to argue it's no stronger than the Elf.
  2. Design a fodder monster that moves 10 squares like a Goblin. Put four of them in a room on the blank map, and play it with a friend as the Zargon player.
  3. Write a named villain's stat box for the end of your quest, and decide which Dread spell (if any) it gets.
