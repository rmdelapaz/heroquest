Lore: The World of HeroQuest

This page sums up what the official books say about the HeroQuest world and marks clearly where fan elaboration takes over.

About this page: the official quest books sketch the HeroQuest world in a few paragraphs of backstory and short quest introductions. This page mixes those official names (Mentor, the Loretome, Zargon, Rogar, Sir Ragnar, Prince Magnus, Ulag, Grak, the Witch Lord, Barak Tor, Kaba Karn, Darkfire Pass and others) with fan elaboration written for this guide. Anything not in the books is labelled as an interpretation, so you can keep it or drop it at your table.

"Long ago, when the world was younger, a barbarian prince carried the Star of the West against the darkness. Now the darkness stirs again, and Mentor looks for heroes..."

What the Official Books Establish

The 2021 Avalon Hill Quest Book opens with a short history told by Mentor, the sage who guards the Loretome, his great book of lore. Everything else in the setting hangs off a handful of names:

  • Zargon was once Mentor's apprentice. He turned away and pledged allegiance to the great powers of Dread. In play he is the villain, and the player who runs the monsters takes his role.
  • Rogar, a barbarian prince, bore the Star of the West in an earlier age. His companions were Durgin the dwarf, Ladril, an elven fighter-mage from Elethorn, and Telor the wizard. Together they drove Zargon's forces back, but did not destroy them.
  • The King rules the realm the heroes defend. Sir Ragnar is one of the King's most powerful knights, and Prince Magnus pays the heroes' rewards.
  • Zargon's servants include Ulag the Orc Warlord, his son Grak, Verag the gargoyle and the Witch Lord of Barak Tor.
  • Heroes who finish all 14 core quests become Sovereign Knights.
Classic edition (1989–1990): the 1989 UK/European game calls the villain Morcar; the 1990 North American edition renamed him Zargon, and the 2021 game kept Zargon. The classic text also speaks of the Emperor rather than the King, of Chaos rather than Dread, and of Fimir where the 2021 game has Abominations. In the 2026 Wizards of Morcar pack, Morcar is a separate figure whose identity the heroes uncover, so the two names are not simply interchangeable there.

The Four Schools of Hero Magic

The one piece of "world structure" the rules spell out is magic. Heroes draw on four elemental schools, Air, Earth, Fire and Water, each with three spells. Zargon has his own deck of twelve Dread spells, which he gives to particular monsters as each quest directs.

Air, Earth, Fire and Water: how the schools are shared

Calling them "the four winds of magic" or giving each element a personality (steady Earth, restless Air, passionate Fire, adaptable Water) is fan flavour; the books simply name the schools and their spells. The full spell list and draft advice live on Magic & Spells.

The Long Struggle

The books frame the conflict plainly: Mentor and the King's realm on one side, Zargon and Dread on the other, with the heroes in the middle doing the actual work underground. Fans sometimes read more into it, for example a cosmic balance between order and chaos, or Zargon as a tragic fallen pupil. Those readings can add depth to a campaign, but they are interpretations; see Lore: Cosmic Forces for one of them, labelled as such.

The Wider World: Fan Elaboration

The quest books describe places only as far as a quest needs them. Everything in this section is invented for this guide to help a Zargon player set a scene; none of it is official.

An Older Realm in Pieces

One popular way to read the setting is that the King's realm is the surviving heart of something older and larger. Ruined towers, half-forgotten tombs and abandoned dwarven halls make sense if many generations built and fell before the heroes arrived. (In the classic edition's text this realm is ruled by an Emperor; the 2021 books say the King.)

The Borderlands

Between settled land and Zargon's strongholds lie wild border country where the King's law is thin. It is a handy explanation for why monsters keep turning up within a day's march of a village, and why a reward from Prince Magnus is worth the risk.

The Call to Adventure

The official heroes come from very different places. The core box has the Barbarian, Dwarf, Elf and Wizard; the Avalon Hill packs add the Knight, Rogue, Monk, Warlock, Bard, Druid, Berserker and Explorer. What unites them in the books is simple: Mentor needs them, and Zargon's forces are on the move again. Meet them all on The Heroes.

"Every shadow might hide an adventure, and every ruin a legend. Mentor's book is still open; the next page is yours to write."
