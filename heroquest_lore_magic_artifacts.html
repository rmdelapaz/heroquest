Lore: Magic & Artifacts

This page covers where magic comes from in HeroQuest, how it behaves at the table, and the few legendary relics the books name.

About this page: the official quest books sketch the world's magic briefly; most of what they say is in the rules and on the cards. This page mixes official names (Mentor, the Loretome, Zargon, Rogar, Sir Ragnar, Prince Magnus, Ulag, Grak, the Witch Lord, Barak Tor, Kaba Karn, Darkfire Pass and others) with fan elaboration, which is labelled.

"A spell is a promise you can only keep once. Cast it well, because it will not come back until the quest is over."

Magic in the Official Game

HeroQuest's magic is defined almost entirely by how it plays:

  • Four hero schools. Air, Earth, Fire and Water, three spells each. The Wizard picks one school, the Elf picks one of the remaining three, and the Wizard takes the last two, so the Wizard holds 9 spells and the Elf 3.
  • Once per quest. Each spell can be cast once, then it is set aside until the quest ends. Casting a spell is the hero's action for the turn, and the target must be in line of sight.
  • Dread spells. Zargon has twelve. The quest notes say which monsters may cast which spells; some are held back for quest packs and custom quests.
  • Mind Points. The rulebook calls Mind "wisdom, intelligence, and resistance to magical influence". In the classic card text, a hero hit by Sleep, Fear or Command rolls one red die per Mind Point and breaks free on a 6.
  • Potions. Drinking one is not an action and can be done at any time, even to stay alive at 0 Body Points.
Classic edition (1989–1990): Zargon's (or Morcar's) spells were Chaos spells. The original European game had none at all; the 1990 North American edition added them, and the 2021 game keeps them as Dread spells.

The Elemental Schools

The schools and spell names are official (names as printed on the classic cards). The one-line character sketch for each is fan flavour.

Air

Swift Wind, Tempest, Genie. Flavour: quick and unpredictable, the school of movement and sudden reversals.

Earth

Heal Body, Rock Skin, Pass Through Rock. Flavour: patient and protective, the school that keeps heroes alive.

Fire

Fire of Wrath, Courage, Ball of Flame. Flavour: passionate and destructive, the school that ends fights.

Water

Water of Healing, Sleep, Veil of Mist. Flavour: adaptable and subtle, healing one moment and hiding the party the next.

An older version of this page added "Spirit" and "Void" schools. Those are not in the game. The official additions are the Protection, Detection and Darkness schools in Wizards of Morcar (2026), which can replace the classic schools while the Elf and Wizard still take one and three sets. Full spell rules are on Magic & Spells.

Where Magic Comes From in Play

Sources of magic and when you use them

The Avalon Hill packs widen the toolbox: the Warlock's Demonform (an extra attack die and immunity to traps), the Bard's songs (Lullaby, Healing Song, Inspiring Tale), the Druid's animal allies, the Monk's elemental unarmed fighting, and alchemy (brewing potions) in Rise of the Dread Moon.

Legendary Relics

The official story names very few relics, which makes each one matter.

The Spirit Blade

The core campaign's great artifact. After the heroes first reach the Witch Lord's barrow at Barak Tor, Quest 13, Quest for the Spirit Blade, sends them to find it before they return in Quest 14.

The Star of the West

Rogar the barbarian prince bore the Star of the West in the earlier war against Zargon. The books say little more about it, and it belongs to the backstory rather than to any card, which leaves plenty of room for a custom campaign.

The Loretome

Mentor's great book of lore. Not a treasure to be found, but the most important book in the setting.

Beyond these, the 2021 core box has 14 artifact cards: rare weapons, armour and magical items that quests hand out as special rewards. They cannot be bought from the armory. See Treasure & Equipment for how they fit with gold and gear. (The old version of this page described a "Crown of Kings", an "Armband of Strength" and "Borin's Compass"; those were invented and have been removed.)

A Fan Reading: The Maker's Mark

This section is interpretation, invented for this guide. Because artifacts are so rare, it helps to give each one a history when it appears: who made it, who carried it last and what happened to them. A single sentence read aloud ("this blade was last seen at Barak Tor") turns a card into a story beat, and costs nothing in rules.
