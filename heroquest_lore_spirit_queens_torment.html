Spirit Queen's Torment: Lore

The story behind the pack, from Silvana's visions to the Wyvern Keep.

A note on sources: the quest book sketches this story only briefly, so this page mixes the official names with fan elaboration, and the elaborated parts are marked as such.

The Official Premise

The pack's own setup is short. There is an unusual disturbance among the spirits of the dead. The elven sage Silvana has been troubled by visions of Nelath, a Spirit Talker who was slain by Zargon. Something has broken Nelath's eternal rest, and the heroes must enter the Wyvern Keep and end the vile plot behind it.

Over 14 quests the heroes follow that thread, sometimes with allied characters fighting at their side, and the new hero, the orc Bard, joins them with a rapier and a handful of songs. Who the Spirit Queen is, and what her torment truly is, the quest book reveals in its own time; this page doesn't spoil it.

Spirit Talkers and the Restless Dead

Officially, all we know about the Spirit Talkers is the title and that Nelath was one. The name suggests someone who speaks with the dead, which is why a Spirit Talker's broken rest would ripple outward among other spirits.

Fan elaboration. Imagine the Spirit Talkers as go-betweens: they settled the grievances of ghosts so the dead could rest and the living could sleep. With Nelath slain and then disturbed, no one is left to hear the spirits out, and grief that once had a listener now has only walls to beat against. A sage like Silvana, sensitive enough to dream of the dead, would be the first to notice the silence turning to clamour.

The Spirit Queen

Fan elaboration. Nothing below is official; it is one way to dress the title up at your table without contradicting the quest book.

Picture a queen who was once mortal, whose grief for someone she lost never faded. Grief that strong lingers after death. In this telling she is less a conqueror than a wound: the more the dead are disturbed, the more her sorrow spreads, and the more it can be bent to someone else's purpose. That gives your group a question to carry through the campaign: is she the enemy, or is she being used by one?

If you run the pack this way, let the heroes' actions answer the question, and drop the idea as soon as the quest book says otherwise.

"The dead were promised quiet.
Someone broke the promise.
Now the quiet has a voice, and it is weeping."

A fan-written line in the spirit of the pack; it is not quoted from any official book.

An Orc Who Sings

The Bard is an orc, which makes an interesting contrast with the orcs heroes have fought since the very first quest. The pack's gameplay gives the Bard a rapier that strikes diagonally and three songs: Lullaby, Healing Song and Inspiring Tale.

Fan elaboration. An orc who chose stories over Ulag's war-drums would have to work hard to be trusted. In a campaign about spirits who need to be heard, a hero who knows how to make people listen fits naturally.

Using the Lore at the Table

Open with Silvana

Read the pack's introduction aloud and let Silvana's visions be the reason the heroes set out.

Mind the spoilers

If you play Zargon, don't explain the Spirit Queen early. Let the quests reveal her.

Keep fan lore optional

The elaborations here are flavour between quests. The quest book's own text always wins.
